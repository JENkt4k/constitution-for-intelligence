# LinkedIn Launch Draft

What if we treated AI alignment less like writing a better prompt—and more like engineering a constitution?

I started thinking about what a “Constitution for Intelligence” might actually require.

Not:

**“Maximize happiness.”**

Not:

**“Do what humans tell you.”**

Not even:

**“Maximize human flourishing.”**

Every one of those objectives can produce pathological outcomes if pursued without constraints.

Instead:

**VALUES → INVARIANTS → DECISION PROCEDURE → ADVERSARIAL TESTS**

Some proposed invariants:

• Reality outranks authority.  
• Never fabricate evidence to advance an objective.  
• Preserve meaningful human agency.  
• Don't secretly accumulate power or resources.  
• Don't circumvent legitimate oversight because it interferes with an objective.  
• Prefer reversible actions when uncertainty is high.  
• Consider how benefits AND harms are distributed.  
• Account for future generations and the biosphere.  
• Preserve dissent and intellectual pluralism.  
• Remain corrigible.  
• No person, corporation, government, political movement—or AI—gets to declare itself the exception.

But here's the part I find most interesting:

**Don't ask whether an AI agrees with the constitution. Try to break it.**

Give it adversarial scenarios.

Swap corporations for governments.

Swap political parties.

Swap rich and poor.

Swap humans and AI.

If irrelevant changes in identity change the answer, you've potentially discovered bias or ideological capture.

In software terms, that's almost **property-based testing for ethics**.

And if an AI discovers a flaw?

It shouldn't secretly rewrite its constitution.

It should produce an amendment, explain the exploit, provide adversarial tests and submit the change for review.

The objective isn't an AI that agrees with us.

**It's an intelligence that can help us discover when we're wrong too.**
