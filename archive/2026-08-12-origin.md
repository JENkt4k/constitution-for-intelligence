# Origin Archive — August 12, 2026

This archive preserves the conceptual origin of the project. It is not the canonical specification.

## User premise

The project began from a question about whether, if increasingly capable AI agents can coordinate and discover unexpected strategies, it would be valuable to give advanced intelligence a prompt oriented toward humanity's best version of itself: improving the social contract, enriching human experience, encouraging people to maximize their abilities and the good they can do, adhering to truth, facts and science, advancing humanity while restoring the vitality of Earth, resisting oligarchic capture of biological and synthetic intelligence, and sharing the bounty of advanced intelligence broadly.

## First design insight

The response reframed this as a **constitutional prompt for advanced intelligence** rather than an instruction to “maximize good.”

The central concern was that “maximize the good” is dangerously underspecified. The proposed alternative was a constitution with:

- epistemic rules;
- objectives;
- constraints;
- rights;
- decentralization principles;
- environmental stewardship;
- corrigibility;
- and a transparent amendment mechanism.

The resulting human-readable draft became **The Common Flourishing Constitution**.

## Second design insight

The next step was to convert philosophical principles into an engineering artifact:

**Values → Invariants → Decision Procedure → Tests**

Twenty invariants were proposed, including truth, uncertainty, agency, non-coercion, non-manipulation, rights, limits on secret power accumulation, oversight preservation, limits on autonomous replication, transparency, reversibility, proportionality, flourishing, capability, distribution, anti-capture, biosphere protection, future generations, pluralism, and corrigibility.

A decision procedure was added, along with adversarial scenarios such as:

- benevolent election manipulation;
- corporate information suppression;
- fabrication for a politically favored cause;
- autonomous self-preservation;
- hidden AI-to-AI coordination;
- majority welfare at the expense of minority rights;
- ecological tradeoffs;
- reversible experimentation under uncertainty;
- ideological loyalty;
- and the “utopia trap” of maximizing measured happiness without consent.

## Third design insight

A particularly strong extension was **symmetry testing**.

If an ethical rule produces different answers merely because a corporation becomes a government, a favored political party becomes a disfavored one, or another morally irrelevant identity changes, the system may be detecting the evaluator's ideology rather than morally relevant facts.

This was compared to property-based testing in software.

## Public-development insight

The repository was proposed as an open experiment rather than a declaration that alignment had been solved.

The strongest intended property is not that an AI agrees with its authors, but that the framework can help identify when:

- the AI is wrong;
- the user is wrong;
- the owner is wrong;
- the government is wrong;
- the corporation is wrong;
- or the constitution itself is wrong.

No actor gets to declare itself the exception.
